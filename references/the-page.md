# The page

`squad/business.md`, whole, in this shape and nothing else. Other agents read the headings by exact
string, WHO by its line 1 and PRICE by its line 1.

```
# The offer · <founder name> · <YYYY-MM-DD>

## THE SENTENCE
**<what you sell, to whom, for what result, 1 sentence a stranger could repeat>**

## WHO
<line 1: the buyer, 1 kind of business or person>
<line 2: where he can be found>

## THE PROBLEM
<his problem, in his words, pointing at a buyer-words line>

## WHAT HE PAYS NOW
<what the sellers below charge, as a fact, e.g. "$1,500 to $3,000 setup at 7 of 12">

## THE SHAPE
<website | content | consulting program | software>

## THE PROMISE
<what he gets and by which day; only what the founder controls; never money he will make>
What I stand behind: <what gets delivered, and the day>

## PRICE
<line 1: $ one number, then one-time | a month | setup + $N a month>

## BUYER WORDS
1. "<verbatim>" · <where> · <link> · <date>

## WHO ALREADY SELLS TO HIM
| Seller | What they sell | Price | Link |
|---|---|---|---|

## WHY THIS ONE
<1 line: why this idea over the one in answer 5, with the counts; or "the only idea">
```

## The law

- These 10 headings, these strings, this order. No other heading, note, stamp or line.
- Every line traces to an answer or a receipt: a founder's answer, a BUYER WORDS line or a row of the
  sellers table. Nothing invented.
- 1 page, 1 offer, 1 price. A range only in WHAT HE PAYS NOW and the sellers table, because they report what others charge.
- Numerals, plain words, no em dashes.
- A heading sits empty only mid-run, where the run picks up. A finished page has no empty heading.

## THE SENTENCE

Bold, 1 sentence: what you sell, to whom, for what result. A stranger hears it once and can say it
back. The result is one the buyer names, in BUYER WORDS or in answer 1, and never an amount of money.
What the thing does is not a result, by the same test as THE PROMISE: `answers missed calls and books
appointments` is what it does. Answer 1 naming only what it does counts as no result.
Neither names one: ask 1 line and wait, `What does your buyer get out of it, in 1 short line?`
It never copies a seller's words. Its noun is the `product word` row in `.claude/squad-roots.md`.

## WHO

Line 1: the buyer of the chosen idea, 1 kind of business or person (`family dentists`, never `small
businesses`). The founder's answer, else the answer to the extra question, else the buyer most of
the sellers name.

Line 2: where he can be found. How the people who paid found the founder (answer 4) when someone has
paid, else `not found yet`. After the dig, the subreddits, YouTube channels and forums the buyer words came from
go on line 2 too, in place of `not found yet`. A review site is not where he is found and stays off line 2.

## THE PROBLEM

1 or 2 lines about the problem THE SENTENCE fixes. Only who, then quoted words copied from the
buyer-words line each one ends on: `<who>: "<verbatim>" (buyer words 2)`. `<who>` is the role his
review prints (`Dentist, President`), else WHO line 1 as written. No other word outside the quote
marks. The quote may be a shorter piece of that line, cut by rule 4 of "What counts as a quote" in
`references/the-dig.md`, and never the piece where he says the problem does not bother him.

Pick the buyer-words lines about the problem itself, before he bought anything. A line about a tool
he tried and what went wrong goes here only when no such line exists.

No buyer words about that problem: 1 phrase in quote marks, copied letter for letter from the
sellers' pages that print it exactly (`"missed calls"`, never a feature like `24/7 answering`), the
phrase the most pages print, ending `(7 of 10 sellers' pages)`, the count of pages whose word-for-word
problem sentences (taken in step 3 of part 1 of `references/the-dig.md`) hold that exact
phrase as whole words (`missed calls` does not count for `missed call`). No phrase printed on 3 or
more pages: quote 1 page's line and end `(<seller>'s page)`.

## WHAT HE PAYS NOW

1 line off the sellers table: the lowest and the highest of those same per-seller numbers (PRICE, rule 3).
Each range carries the count of sellers priced on that term, out of the number read:
`$30 to $999 a month at 5 of 9`. A seller priced only on another term (a `$37 one-time` pack) is not
in that count. A free plan or a free tier is never the lowest. Setup plus monthly: each range carries
its own count, `$199 to $500 setup at 3 of 10, $109 to $500 a month at 6 of 10`.

## THE SHAPE

1 of these 4, picked by the main thing the buyer receives. Every offer maps to exactly 1, and it
decides what /execution-genesis-demo builds.

| Shape | The founder |
|---|---|
| website | builds or fixes a site |
| content | makes posts, videos, images or ads |
| consulting program | runs a program that takes clients through stages, his own or one he sets up for a coach |
| software | builds a tool, an app, an automation or an AI agent |

## THE PROMISE

Line 1: what he gets, and by which day after he pays. Only what the founder controls. Never money,
a number of bookings or calls, rankings, followers or any result that depends on the buyer or the
market. What the thing does (`answers his calls and books into his calendar`) is the delivery, not a
result.

Line 2: `What I stand behind: <what gets delivered>, by day <N>.` A delivery and a day, never a result.

The day: answer 3's, when the offer is answer 1's idea and answer 3 gives one. Else the middle
delivery time of the sellers in the table that publish one, in days, the lower end of each (`1 to 2
weeks` is 7, `48 to 72 hours` is 2); a delivery time is how long until the thing made for him is in
his hands, and `immediate access`, `instant`, how fast a tool renders, or a turnaround per video or per edit counted from when he sends his footage is not one; with an even count, the lower of the 2 middle ones. A seller's
delivery time counts only when it belongs to the plan in its Price cell and is printed in that row's
What they sell cell. None publishes one: ask the founder 1 line and wait, `By which day after he pays does he have it?`

```
A 5-page site with online booking, live by day 14 after he pays.
What I stand behind: the site, live, by day 14.
```

## PRICE

Line 1 is 1 of `$<N> one-time`, `$<N> a month` or `$<N> setup + $<N> a month`. When the number came
from the sellers table, `(the middle of <N> published prices)` follows it.

Line 2, only when there is a monthly fee: what the monthly fee keeps running.

The number:

1. Answer 3's number as given, when the offer is answer 1's idea and answer 3 gives 1 number, or 2
   numbers, 1 for setup and 1 a month (`$<N> setup + $<N> a month`), even when it sits above every
   seller's price.
2. The offer is answer 1's idea and answer 3 gives a range: the table's middle price when it falls inside that range, else the end of
   the range closest to it.
3. Otherwise the middle published price among the sellers of the closest thing. Take the prices on
   the term most of them use, sort them, and take the middle one; with an even count, the lower of
   the 2 middle ones. Setup plus monthly: the middle setup and the middle monthly, each on its own.
   Each seller counts once, at its lowest price on that term: `from $109 a month` is 109, `$500 to
   $700 a month` is 500, 3 plans is the cheapest paid plan open to the buyer in WHO line 1 (a plan only
   for 3 or more locations does not count for 1 office). A $0 plan is not a price. A price billed only yearly counts at the
   month-to-month price when the page shows one, else as printed. Only prices in the currency most
   of them use count. When half or more of those sellers also publish a setup fee, line 1 is
   `$<N> setup + $<N> a month`.

Never a range, never "from", never 2 options.

```
$2,500 one-time
```

```
$400 a month (the middle of 7 published prices)
The $400 a month keeps the ads running.
```

## BUYER WORDS

Up to 10 lines, written the way part 2 of `references/the-dig.md` says. A line is removed, never
reworded.

## WHO ALREADY SELLS TO HIM

The sellers table from part 1 of `references/the-dig.md`: the chosen offer's sellers only, published
prices first.

## WHY THIS ONE

1 line.

- Answer 1 was "nothing yet" and there was no answer 5: `what 6 of 10 sellers to <buyer> sell`.
- Otherwise only 1 idea: `the only idea`.
- 2 ideas: which one won, with both counts and the bar, which is 2 times answer 1's count.
  - Answer 1's idea won: `<idea> over <other idea>: 7 sellers with a published price against 2, and <other idea> needed 14.`
  - Answer 5's idea won: `<idea> over <other idea>: 8 sellers with a published price against 3, and it needed 6.` Answer 1's idea had none: `<idea> over <other idea>: 4 sellers with a published price against none.`
  - Answer 5's read stopped at 5: `<idea> over <other idea>: 6 sellers with a published price against 2 of 5 read, and <other idea> needed 12, more than the 10 it could read.`
